# Third-party assets

The furniture models in `assets/models/block/` are adapted from the
projects below, and the textures in `assets/textures/block/` are
copied from them. The pots are the one exception and have their own section at
the end of this file, because they are art without a declared licence. Models are generated by `tools/furniture-gen` rather than
edited by hand: each one is its source model with the parent chain collapsed,
bundled texture paths rewritten into the `lunasmp` namespace, and, where a
piece needs it, a rotation baked in (a wall piece stands on the floor) or the
geometry cut into a post and four arms (a trellis joins up like a fence).
Textures referenced as `minecraft:` are vanilla and ship with the game.

MIT requires that the copyright notice and permission notice travel with the
work, which is what this file is for. Each project's full licence text is
reproduced below.

## Floral Enchantment (Cozary) — NOT ADOPTED

https://modrinth.com/mod/floral-enchantment · https://github.com/Cozary/Floral-Enchantment

The flowering vines, the flowering mossy cobblestone and stone bricks, the
growable bushes, the long planter and the flower crowns are that mod's ideas.
**None of its files are here**, and that is deliberate: its `LICENSE` reads
`All Rights Reserved` on every branch of its repository, including the
`1.21.11` one, and Modrinth lists it the same way.

A source drop of it can carry an MIT `LICENSE` and `license=MIT` in
`gradle.properties`. Those are not the author's: they are left over from the
multiloader template the mod is built on, whose `fabric.mod.json` it still
ships pointing at `FabricMC/fabric-example-mod`. The file does not match what
the author publishes, so it is not a grant.

So every one of those families is drawn here from scratch, in
`tools/furniture-gen/flora.ts` and `compose.ts`: our own geometry over
`minecraft:` texture references, which the client already has and which we
therefore never redistribute. That is why they read as vanilla flowers growing
on vanilla stone rather than as painted variants, and it is why one catalog
entry serves twenty-six flowers instead of twenty-six drawn sheets.

## NadBlocks (Nadwey) — NOT ADOPTED

https://github.com/Nadwey/nadblocks

The hazard block, the drain grate and the ceiling lamp are that addon's list of
objects. **None of its files are here.** Its repository carries no licence at
all - no LICENSE file, no terms in the README, and the GitHub API reports none
for either the original or the fork that was pointed at
(`awakcon1234/nadblocks`, which adds Vietnamese translations and nothing else).
By the rule this repository already applies to Fantasy's Furniture and to
Gardener's Dream, a missing licence is the most restrictive state rather than
the least, and a fork cannot grant what upstream never granted.

So all three are built from scratch in `tools/furniture-gen/industrial.ts`: the
stripes, the steel, the shaft floor and the frosted panel are painted there, and
the geometry is composed beside them over one vanilla texture
(`minecraft:block/deepslate_tiles`, for the walls of the drain shaft) that the
client already has.

## The signs — entirely ours

The thirty signs are drawn pixel by pixel in `tools/furniture-gen/signart.ts`:
a five-by-seven capital alphabet, fourteen pictograms as character grids, and
the shapes, fields and borders composed from those.

The shapes themselves are the standard public pictograms - ISO 7010 for the
safety and electrical signs, the Vienna Convention for the road signs. Those are
conventions rather than anybody's artwork, which is why a sign set can be drawn
at all; what is not reused is anybody's *rendering* of them. Nothing here is
traced from or sampled out of another project's file.

Three sign mods were read for what a set like this should contain and for
nothing else. **LandOfSignals** (`LandOfRails/LandOfSignals`) is All Rights
Reserved outright. **ClickSigns** (`Clickism/ClickSigns`) and **Ultimate Road
Signs and Signals** are GPL-3.0, which would have allowed a port, but the art
here was faster to draw than to adapt and it matches the rest of the pack.

## Redstone Gauges and Switches (Stefan Wilhelm / wile) — CONSULTED, NOT COPIED

https://github.com/stfwi/rsgauges · MIT (LICENSE at the repository root, all branches)

The user pointed at this mod as a reference for the network instruments, and
its licence would have allowed a port. It was read for design language only:
the industrial O/I rotary switch marking and the panel-meter styling informed
the shapes drawn here. **None of its files travelled.** Its gauges also work

if copying had been the plan. Every face, needle, casing and switch texture is
painted in `tools/furniture-gen/gaugeart.ts`.

## Gardener's Dream (Gurkis) — UNRESOLVED

The datapack the pots are ported from. Only the art travelled: the potting
itself is written from scratch here as a Nova tile entity, and none of the
datapack's own files are shipped or adapted.

That art is sixty player-head skins, which the datapack carries as base64
profile properties on its stonecutting recipes and which the generator
downloads from Mojang's texture server. **The datapack declares no licence.**
It ships no LICENSE file, states no terms in `pack.mcmeta`, and credits only
its author, `@GurkisDev`. By the rule this repository already applies to
Fantasy's Furniture, that is the most restrictive state rather than the least,
and these textures should not go to a production server until somebody has
asked the author.

Removing them is one edit and one generator run: drop `POTS` from the catalog's
`CATALOG` array. The blocks, the plant table and everything that makes a pot
hold a plant are ours and stay.

## The crops (ours, over Stardew Valley's published crop data)

Every crop sprite in `assets/textures/block/*_crop_stage*.png` and every crop
item sprite is drawn in `tools/furniture-gen/crops.ts` from eight plant
silhouettes and a per-crop palette. **Nothing of ConcernedApe's art is copied,
traced or sampled**, and no file of the game's is read: the pixels here are
built from primitives the same way the signs and the flowering vines are.

What is taken is the game's crop *data*, read off the Stardew Valley Wiki's
[Crops](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Crops) page on 2026-09-13: which crops
exist, what season each belongs to, how many days each growth stage lasts,
which keep producing after the first harvest and how often, how many items a
harvest gives, and what each sells for. Those are facts about a published
game rather than expression, and they are the reason a parsnip here takes four
days and an ancient fruit takes twenty-eight.

Crops Minecraft already grows (wheat, potato, carrot, beetroot, melon,
pumpkin) are deliberately absent, and so are the three Stardew flowers vanilla
has its own version of (poppy, tulip, sunflower), which the flora catalog
already ships.

Two rules are ours rather than the game's, both written down in
`crops/CropBehavior.kt`: unwatered farmland halves growth instead of stopping
it, and a crop that does not regrow returns a seed when it is picked. The
game's answers there (no growth at all, and a seed maker) both lose a player's
farm to something they cannot see from inside Minecraft.
